How the model works

Everything runs in your browser; there is no server. Each time it's your turn, the page estimates how often your hand wins, compares that to the price of calling, and applies a short rule to pick an action. The heatmap is a picture of those same two numbers.

  1. Equity, by simulation

    The engine deals 1,200 random hands to the villain and random cards for the rest of the board, then counts how often you win. Ties count as half. That win rate is your equity. With 1,200 samples it is accurate to about ±3 points.

  2. Pot odds: the price

    call ÷ (pot + call) is the minimum equity a call needs to break even. Calling $20 into a $60 pot needs 20 ÷ 80 = 25%.

  3. Decision rule

    Equity and pot odds go through the thresholds in the table below. The result is the suggested action, plus the dollar EV of calling.

  4. Action field

    An eight-term function Ψ is drawn over every equity × pot-odds pair, with your spot marked. It shows where your spot sits relative to the fold, call and raise regions.

The decision rule

The suggestion comes from these four lines. The value bar rises 5 points per street (65% pre-flop, 70% flop, 75% turn, 80% river) because later bets are larger and harder to recover from.

Situation Condition Suggestion
Any equity > value bar Bet 60% of the pot, or Raise if facing a bet
No bet to face otherwise Check
Facing a bet equity > pot odds − 5 and equity > 30% Call
Facing a bet otherwise Fold

EV of calling = equity × (pot + call) − call

The action field Ψ

Ψ adds eight terms over the plane of equity e and pot odds p (both 0–1). Each term favors one kind of action in one region. ê and p̂ are your current equity and pot odds, s is the street (0–3), a is bet ÷ pot, and h is your made hand on a 0–1 scale.

Ψ(e, p) = V + R + C + F + B + S + J + Γ

Term Formula What it does
V Your spot 65·exp(−3(e−ê)² − 1.5(p−p̂)²) A peak centered on where you are.
R Raise 50·e2.5·exp(−2(p−0.4)²)·(1+0.3s) Grows steeply with equity and on later streets.
C Call 35·exp(−5(e−0.45)²)·(1−p)·exp(−(p−p̂)²) A ridge around 45% equity that fades as calls get expensive.
F Fold −20·(1−e)²·p·exp(−0.5e²) Negative where equity is low and the price is high.
B Bluff 18·(1−e)2.5·a·exp(−3(p−0.6)²) Weak hands facing large bets; scales with bet size.
S Street 8·s·e(1−e)·sin(πp) Adds weight to medium hands as the hand goes on.
J Break-even jump 15·tanh(8(e−p̂))·exp(−2(p−p̂)²) A sharp step where equity crosses the price of calling.
Γ Made hand 10·h·e·exp(−0.5(p−0.5)²) Rewards hands that are already made (pairs, straights…).

The heatmap is re-colored from its own minimum (blue) to maximum (red) every time it is drawn, so colors show where your spot sits relative to the rest of the plane. Ψ is a visualization: the suggestion comes from the decision rule above, not from Ψ.

How the villain plays

The villain runs its own 800-hand simulation on its cards. It cannot see yours.

  • Nobody has bet: it bets if its equity is above 60%. Between 40% and 60% it bets 30% of the time. Otherwise it checks. Bets are 50–80% of the pot.
  • Facing a bet: above 55% equity it raises 40% of the time and calls otherwise. Below that it calls when its equity beats the pot odds by 5 points. Otherwise it folds, except for a random call 8% of the time.

Assumptions

  • Equity is measured against a random hand. It does not narrow the villain's range based on how the villain has bet.
  • Heads-up no-limit: blinds $5/$10, both players start every hand with $500. You are always the small blind and act first on every street.
  • Each simulation is fresh, so equity can move by a point or two between identical spots.
